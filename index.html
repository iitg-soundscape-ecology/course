Why study soundscapes?

A soundscape is the composition of sounds in an environment. It can reveal ecological activity, human activity and aspects of social and linguistic life. In speech technologies, however, sounds outside the target voice are often treated as noise. This module asks what information may be lost when that design choice is made.

Learning outcomes

  • Explain the idea and ecological relevance of soundscapes.
  • Distinguish natural, human and anthropogenic sound events using the project's terminology.
  • Describe the EAR recording system and its data-retrieval constraints.
  • Interpret basic waveforms, spectrograms and time-based annotations.
  • Explain how supervised classification and bird-species detection can support soundscape analysis.
  • Interpret soundscape indices cautiously and identify their limitations.
  • Evaluate responsible-computing questions involving energy, data, privacy, linguistic diversity and environmental stewardship.
How to use this module: Work through the lessons in sequence. Each lesson ends with a short self-check. The final assessment is available at any time; completing all lessons first is recommended.
