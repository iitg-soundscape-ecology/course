Responsible Computing in Environmental AI

Fairness, privacy, community participation, sustainability and accountability

Learning objectives

By the end of this chapter, you should be able to

  • Identify ethical and social issues in acoustic monitoring.
  • Explain dataset and model bias.
  • Assess privacy and community considerations.
  • Describe ways to reduce computational costs.

Part 1 · Guided reading — 15 minutes

Representation and bias

Models may perform differently across habitats, regions, species and recording devices. Under-represented ecosystems may be misclassified or missed.

Privacy and community

Environmental recordings can capture speech or human activity. Consider notice, consent where appropriate, data minimisation, access controls, retention and local expectations.

Sustainability

Continuous recording, storage, transmission and inference consume resources. Sampling design, efficient models, local processing and selective transmission can reduce impact.

Accountability

Document design decisions, model limitations, error rates, data provenance and intended uses. Provide channels for affected communities to raise concerns.

Part 2 · Practical activity — 30 minutes

Conduct a responsible deployment review

  1. Review a proposed soundscape monitoring deployment.
  2. Assess privacy, community participation, ecological sensitivity, model bias and energy use.
  3. Rate each risk low, medium or high.
  4. Propose a mitigation and monitoring measure for each high-risk issue.
Suggested output: A short written response, annotated diagram, calculation, plot or completed worksheet.
