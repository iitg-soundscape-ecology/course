  • Select suitable plots for acoustic and classification data.

  • Avoid misleading comparisons.

Part 1 · Guided reading — 15 minutes

Visualising sound

Waveforms and spectrograms show signal structure; time-series plots show index variation; heatmaps can show activity by hour and date; bar charts compare categories.

Spatial context

Maps can show recording locations, habitat types and differences among sites. Location data may be sensitive, especially for threatened species or private land.

Good practice

Label axes and units, show uncertainty where possible, distinguish detections from confirmed observations, and avoid comparing incompatible scales.

Project case study · From CSV output to a soundscape dashboard

Figure 7 from the project report shows an example of the visualization generated from the structured output. It combines summary indicators, a temporal view of soundscape activity, frequently detected bird species and a plain-language summary.

What does the dashboard communicate?

  • Summary cards: counts or proportions of natural and anthropogenic activity.
  • Temporal view: how detected bird, natural and human activity varies over the recording period.
  • Bird list: the most frequently detected candidate species.
  • Plain-language summary: a concise description of the aggregate results.

Practical activity

  1. Load a CSV generated by the Module 10 pipeline.
  2. Calculate recording-level summaries from the window-level rows.
  3. Plot AAI and NSI over time and display BAI or richness as an additional layer.
  4. Generate a ranked list of detected bird species.
  5. Write a short summary that distinguishes model detections from confirmed ecological observations.
Visualization design principle: Show the time period, number of recordings and analysis windows, model/version information and any filtering rules. A dashboard should make the provenance and limitations of its summaries visible.

Part 2 · Practical activity — 30 minutes

Create a soundscape visual summary

  1. Choose one recording or a small set of window-level results.
  2. Create a time-series plot for one index or detection rate.
  3. Create a category or species activity plot.
  4. If location data are available, map sites at suitable precision.
  5. Write a short interpretation and one limitation.
Suggested output: A short written response, annotated diagram, calculation, plot or completed worksheet.
