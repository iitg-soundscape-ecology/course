Soundscape Visualisation and Geospatial Analysis

Exploring temporal and spatial patterns in environmental audio

← Course home60 minutesReading · Practice · Reflection
Learning objectives

By the end of this chapter, you should be able to

  • Select suitable plots for acoustic and classification data.
  • Visualise temporal activity and species detections.
  • Explain how soundscape data can be mapped.
  • Avoid misleading comparisons.

One-hour learning plan

15 minGuided reading
30 minPractical activity
15 minReflection and quiz

Part 1 · Guided reading — 15 minutes

Visualising sound

Waveforms and spectrograms show signal structure; time-series plots show index variation; heatmaps can show activity by hour and date; bar charts compare categories.

Spatial context

Maps can show recording locations, habitat types and differences among sites. Location data may be sensitive, especially for threatened species or private land.

Good practice

Label axes and units, show uncertainty where possible, distinguish detections from confirmed observations, and avoid comparing incompatible scales.

Part 2 · Practical activity — 30 minutes

Create a soundscape visual summary

  1. Choose one recording or a small set of window-level results.
  2. Create a time-series plot for one index or detection rate.
  3. Create a category or species activity plot.
  4. If location data are available, map sites at suitable precision.
  5. Write a short interpretation and one limitation.
Suggested output: A short written response, annotated diagram, calculation, plot or completed worksheet.
