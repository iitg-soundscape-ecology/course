Practical Use of PANNs and BirdNET in Soundscape Analysis

A case study of parallel environmental sound tagging and bird species detection

Learning objectives

By the end of this chapter, you should be able to

  • Explain complementary roles of PANNs and BirdNET.
  • Describe how both models are applied to the same audio windows.
  • Interpret AudioSet labels and BirdNET detections.
  • Explain aggregation of model outputs.

Part 1 · Guided reading — 15 minutes

PANNs

Pretrained Audio Neural Networks, including CNN14, provide general-purpose audio tagging using AudioSet categories. Their outputs indicate a range of environmental sound classes.

BirdNET

BirdNET is designed for bird sound recognition and provides candidate species detections. Results depend on model version, recording quality, geography and threshold.

Parallel analysis

The case-study workflow analyses the same recording in parallel: PANNs supplies broad sound-event labels while BirdNET supplies bird-species detections.

Window-based integration

Recordings are divided into short overlapping analysis windows. Detections from both models are aggregated, broad labels are mapped into sound categories, and one row is stored per analysis window.

Validation

A detection-bearing window is not necessarily an independent vocalisation. Predictions should be checked against audio, field context and expert knowledge.

Part 2 · Practical activity — 30 minutes

Read an integrated PANNs–BirdNET result

  1. Inspect a sample analysis-window record containing PANNs labels, BirdNET species and scores.
  2. Separate broad environmental labels from bird-species detections.
  3. Map PANNs labels into broad sound categories.
  4. Identify one possible overlap or double-counting issue.
  5. Write an interpretation distinguishing model output from confirmed ecological evidence.
Suggested output: A short written response, annotated diagram, calculation, plot or completed worksheet.
