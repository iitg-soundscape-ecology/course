Part 1 · Guided reading — 15 minutes

Responsible computing

Consider not only whether a system works, but whom it benefits, whom it may harm, whose knowledge it represents, and how it affects society and the environment.

Mozilla RCC

The Responsible Computing Challenge supports curricula that help students examine the social and political context of computing and integrate responsibility into computing education. The program aims to educate graduating technologists who will bring holistic thinking to the design of new technologies empowering them to think about the environmental, social and political implications of computing.

Why soundscapes?

Soundscapes are the sounds that we hear around us consisting biological (biophony), physical/natural (geophony), and human-made (anthrophony) sounds. Exposure to a healthy soundscape is vital for the overall mental and physical health of the people in the enrironment. Hence, soundscapes need to be observed and assessed to figure out if the biophony and the anthrophony are balanced or not. Any imbalance may indicate that the environment needs intervention to build a better co-habitable space for humans and other organisms. However, capturing and assessing soundscpaes is not straigtforward. Hence, in this program, we first develop a device to capture the soundscapes and then proceed to categorizing them according to their sources. Finally, using some of the well-known measurers, we provide objective analysis of the soundscpaes.

Part 2 · Practical activity — 30 minutes

Responsibility mapping for a soundscape device

  1. Imagine deploying an autonomous recorder in a forest near a village.
  2. List affected groups: researchers, local residents, forest staff and nearby communities.
  3. For each group, identify one benefit, one risk and one mitigation.
  4. Decide what should be recorded, stored locally, transmitted and shared.
Suggested output: A short written response, annotated diagram, calculation, plot or completed worksheet.
