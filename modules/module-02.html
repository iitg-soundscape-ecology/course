Introduction to Soundscapes and Soundscape Ecology

Understanding environmental sound as an ecological and cultural resource

Learning objectives

By the end of this chapter, you should be able to

  • Define a soundscape and distinguish it from an individual sound event.
  • Explain biophony, geophony and anthrophony.
  • Describe how soundscapes support ecological monitoring.
  • Recognise that interpretation depends on context.

Part 1 · Guided reading — 15 minutes

Soundscape

A soundscape is the acoustic environment as perceived, experienced or studied in context. It includes multiple sound sources interacting across time and space.

Three source groups

Biophony refers to sounds produced by living organisms; geophony includes natural non-biological sounds such as rain and wind; anthrophony refers to sounds generated by human activity.

Soundscape ecology

Soundscape ecology studies patterns in environmental sound and their relationship to organisms, habitats, human activity and environmental change. Recordings provide evidence but do not automatically explain causes.

Part 2 · Practical activity — 30 minutes

Classify a short soundscape

  1. Listen to a one-minute outdoor recording.
  2. Note sound events and approximate start times.
  3. Classify each as biophony, geophony, anthrophony or uncertain/mixed.
  4. Identify one difficult event and explain why.
Suggested output: A short written response, annotated diagram, calculation, plot or completed worksheet.
