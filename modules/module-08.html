AI and Machine Learning for Environmental Sound

Understanding classification models, training data and model uncertainty

Learning objectives

By the end of this chapter, you should be able to

  • Distinguish supervised, unsupervised and transfer learning.
  • Explain CNNs for audio classification.
  • Describe pretrained models and label taxonomies.
  • Interpret confidence scores and errors.

Part 1 · Guided reading — 15 minutes

Machine learning

Supervised learning uses labelled examples; unsupervised learning looks for structure without supplied labels. Transfer learning adapts a pretrained model to a related setting.

Deep learning for audio

Audio can be represented as waveforms or spectrograms. CNNs learn patterns from these representations and can classify sound events.

Pretrained models

Pretrained models reduce the need to build large labelled datasets from scratch, but training data may not represent every habitat, species or recording condition.

Uncertainty

A model score is not automatically a calibrated probability. Thresholds, class imbalance and domain shift affect performance. Human validation remains important.

Part 2 · Practical activity — 30 minutes

Evaluate a model prediction

  1. Inspect environmental sound predictions and scores.
  2. Choose a threshold and note retained detections.
  3. Identify a plausible false positive and false negative.
  4. Explain why a high score does not prove an event occurred.
Suggested output: A short written response, annotated diagram, calculation, plot or completed worksheet.
