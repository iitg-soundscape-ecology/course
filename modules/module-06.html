Soundscape Ecology and Acoustic Indices

Summarising acoustic patterns while recognising what indices cannot tell us

Learning objectives

By the end of this chapter, you should be able to

  • Explain the purpose of acoustic indices.
  • Recognise commonly used indices and broad interpretations.
  • Distinguish an index from a direct ecological measurement.
  • Identify confounds and limitations.

Part 1 · Guided reading — 15 minutes

Why indices?

Acoustic indices summarise aspects of a recording into numerical descriptors. They can help compare patterns across time or sites, but interpretation depends on recording conditions and context.

Examples

ACI describes temporal variation in acoustic energy. ADI and AEI summarise aspects of spectral distribution. Definitions and implementations can vary between software packages.

Interpretation cautions

Wind, rain, traffic, microphone response, gain, habitat structure and schedules can affect index values. An index is not a direct species count or definitive measure of ecosystem health.

Part 2 · Practical activity — 30 minutes

Compare index profiles

  1. Use supplied index values from two sites or periods.
  2. Plot ACI, ADI and AEI separately.
  3. Describe differences without claiming a direct cause.
  4. List additional observations needed for ecological interpretation.
Suggested output: A short written response, annotated diagram, calculation, plot or completed worksheet.
