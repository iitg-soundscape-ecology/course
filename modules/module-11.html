Learning objectives

By the end of this chapter, you should be able to

  • Explain classifier outputs versus derived indicators.
  • Describe AAI, NSI, HDI and BAI in the project.
  • Explain species richness, Shannon diversity and bird detection rate.
  • Identify assumptions in composite indices.

Part 1 · Guided reading — 15 minutes

From labels to categories

Classifier labels can be mapped into broad groups such as biological, anthropogenic and weather-related sounds. Mapping rules should be transparent and version-controlled.

Project indicators

The case-study pipeline computes operational indicators including AAI, NSI, HDI, BAI, species richness, Shannon diversity and Bird Detection Rate.

Interpretation

These are project-defined summaries, not universally validated measures. Values depend on category mapping, thresholds, window duration, overlap and repeated detections.

Diversity measures

Species richness counts distinct detected taxa in a defined unit. Shannon diversity combines richness and relative frequency. Detection-window values are not direct population estimates.

Part 2 · Practical activity — 30 minutes

Interpret a window-level summary

  1. Inspect a small table of categories and bird detections.
  2. Calculate or inspect species richness and Shannon diversity for an interval.
  3. Compare two intervals and describe differences.
  4. List two reasons differences may not reflect ecological change.
Suggested output: A short written response, annotated diagram, calculation, plot or completed worksheet.
